# M0: known short-eclipse recovery, specified before light-curve download

## Question and fixed scope

Can a simple, reproducible short-duration search recover published WD-binary
periods in public TESS 120-second data, with repetition within the sector?
This is a recovery/control experiment, not a discovery scan or a reproduction of
the authors' full pipeline. Metadata alone were inspected before this specification.

Controls are the first three rows of Table 2 in
[Cavalier et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03850v1):

| TIC | Published period (days; grader only) | Fixed metadata-selected sector |
|---|---:|---:|
| 450781262 | 0.09388575 | 99 |
| 53206761 | 0.13280937 | 72 |
| 2041210548 | 0.29092036 | 57 |

These are published controls, not private candidate coordinates. September 12
MAST metadata query returned ten observations across these targets. Choose the
newest available PUBLIC 120-second sector per target. If duplicate observations
within that sector exist, choose greatest numeric obsid, then greatest product
filename in lexical order among SCIENCE LC products ending `_lc.fits`.
Retain all metadata so duplicates are visible. Do not substitute controls on failure.

The paper reports strong confusion risks and ground-based confirmation. Its
short-cadence WD search is prior art, not an unsearched niche; its reported periods
serve only as a check on our independently scanned grid. A subsequent blind search
must establish an incremental data/sample contribution and pixel/independent-survey
confirmation capability first.

## Acquisition and provenance

Use [MAST's documented observation/product API](https://mast.stsci.edu/api/v0/_services.html).
Acquire exactly three selected LC FITS files, at most 20 MB each and 60 MB total;
each request timeout 45 seconds, no automatic indefinite retries. Product listing
may be fetched before file download. Refuse non-PUBLIC metadata, missing SCIENCE
LC products, over-limit responses or malformed FITS. Record selected observation,
URL, bytes, SHA-256, observation time/cadence and software versions. Never execute
downloaded code or scripts. Raw files are ignored; published-control aggregate
results and exact provenance may be committed.

## Fixed search and acceptance

Use finite TIME, PDCSAP_FLUX and positive finite PDCSAP_FLUX_ERR; require QUALITY=0.
Normalize by the median positive flux; **no negative clipping or learned detrending**.
Require at least 1,000 usable points and 10 days baseline; otherwise STOP_COVERAGE.
Use weighted Astropy BoxLeastSquares, positive-depth likelihood objective.
Search periods 0.05--1.0 day, durations 4, 8, 16 minutes. Geometric period step
`dlogP = (4/1440)/(3*baseline_days)` bounds accumulated timing drift between
neighbouring grid periods. Fixed oversample=10 for BLS phase sampling. Evaluate
in bounded chunks, excluding duration/period >0.15. No published period initializes
or refines the search. Highest likelihood admissible solution wins.

Acceptance per control: period within 0.1% of the published period **or its exact
half/double alias (explicitly marked, never silently called exact)**, fitted positive
depth SNR >=10, at least 20 in-eclipse data points and at least 10 distinct observed
events. At the same fixed period/epoch/duration, first and second temporal halves
must each show positive depth at >=5 sigma, with >=5 sampled events. Depth uses
inverse-variance weighted in/out means and their propagated measurement errors.
This deliberately does not claim correlated-noise-adjusted discovery significance.

M0 GO requires **3/3**. Otherwise stop this specification and diagnose; no unknown
search. Passing only permits a separately specified pixel-localization/contamination
and empirical-null stage, not scaling. Synthetic recovery, alias, gap, missing-data,
and failure-status tests accompany the code. Retain every failure and hash the
protocol in results. Wall cap: 10 minutes per control; one bounded run per version.

## Pre-download independent review clarifications

No thresholds changed. Search each duration only on its admissible periods before
maximizing. Ties: earlier duration in the stated list, then smaller period. Flux
and error share the normalization; finite negative flux is retained. Refuse
duplicate timestamps, identity/cadence/time-unit mismatch, and nonfinite results.
Observed events means **sampled predicted eclipse windows**, not individually
significant detections. Temporal halves check persistence of a full-data-selected
model; they are not independent holdouts. The first control's half-period is
outside the fixed range. Metadata/download workers have external 120-second caps,
with 45-second total download deadlines plus byte counting. Search workers have
external 600-second caps. A timeout is recorded as a stop, not a scientific null.
