Verdict: **TARGETED_DASCH_FEASIBILITY_PASS**

This is a successful reproduction of a published positive control, not a new
astronomical discovery.

The verdict applies only to this pilot's frozen light-curve/API gate. It does
not complete the older portfolio M0, whose Mira, faint/crowded, `daschlab`
plate-cutout, and adjacent-epoch image checks remain unexecuted.

This agrees with Luna et al. (2020), who reported an optical bright state that
began in 1938 and lasted about seven years through roughly 1945 before the 1946
nova eruption. The field control makes a gross field-wide calibration shift an
unlikely explanation for this reproduction, but one field star is not a
sufficient systematics model for discovery work.

## What this resolves

- The anonymous DR7 API is usable without a Starglass account at pilot scale.
- The API's JSON-wrapped CSV format can be frozen, checksummed, parsed, and
  audited non-interactively.
- Current daschlab documentation explicitly supports non-interactive work;
  older portfolio notes saying DASCH has no batch mode are obsolete.
- A known century-scale state change survives the documented quality mask and
  a conservative astrometric filter.

## What remains before an unknown-source search

1. Use several magnitude/color/position-matched field controls, not one.
2. Model plate-series and plate-level common modes and quantify the empirical
   false-positive rate on stable stars.
3. Freeze a target population and statistic before inspecting unknown-source
   rankings.
4. Reserve an untouched validation subset of plates or targets.
5. Manually inspect plate cutouts and source splitting for every survivor.
6. Cross-match catalogs and literature before any discovery claim.
7. Keep survivor identities private until an explicit human release decision.

The recommended next lane is a **targeted, preregistered multi-control study**
of one physically motivated source class. A blind all-sky anomaly search is not
yet justified.
