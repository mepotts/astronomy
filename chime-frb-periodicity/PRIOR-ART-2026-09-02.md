# Preliminary prior-art check: Catalog 2 activity cycles

Checked: 2026-09-02

Searches covered combinations of “CHIME/FRB Catalog 2,” “periodicity,” “activity cycle,” “repeaters,” and the catalog size, across arXiv-indexed results and web-indexed NASA ADS results. This is a feasibility-stage search, not a proof of novelty. A fresh ADS query and citation-chain review remain mandatory immediately before any M1 unblinding.

Relevant primary works found:

- [The Second CHIME/FRB Catalog of Fast Radio Bursts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09399) releases the event table and cumulative/integrated exposure products; it does not report a Catalog 2-wide activity-period search.
- [Periodic activity from a fast radio burst source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10275) reports `16.35 +/- 0.15` days for FRB 20180916B and is the M0 positive control.
- [Possible periodic activity in the repeating FRB 121102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03596) reports a roughly 157-day activity cycle, and [an independent campaign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03461) reports `161 +/- 5` days. The scientific premise is therefore not that only one repeating FRB has a published cycle.
- [Discovery of 30 Repeating Fast Radio Burst Sources and Uniform Population Statistics of 80 Repeating Sources from CHIME/FRB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08410) analyzes repeating-source rates and reports several monotonic DM trends; its abstract does not claim a population-wide long-period activity search.
- [Debiasing the Observed Fast Radio Burst Population with the CHIME/FRB Selection Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26334) models selection in burst observables using injected events; it does not supply the needed per-time observing window in its abstract or the Catalog 2 DOI directory.

No primary paper centered on a Catalog 2-wide multi-day activity-cycle search was found in these queries. That leaves the scientific niche plausible, not established. The observing-window failure blocks the project before novelty becomes the limiting gate.
