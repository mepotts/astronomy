Z2  M7's 5-41 uas offset: systematic, or convention mismatch?
==============================================================================
  Panuzzo states the convention in two places, and they are OPPOSITE:
    Table 1 (DR3 single-star parallax)   -- L21 APPLIED (footnote b, 35.4 uas)
    Table 2 (the NSS orbital solutions)  -- NOT applied; the Letter says the
                                            bias 'could not be quantified' for
                                            preliminary NSS solutions (line 262)
  M7 compared its refit parallax against the TABLE 2 value (1.6747 +/- 0.0094),
  which is UNCORRECTED -- so M7's -14.9 uas on BH3 is a comparison of two
  uncorrected parallaxes and is NOT the zero-point.  The zero-point is a
  common offset that cancels in that difference.

  the four BH3 parallaxes, on one scale:
    DR3 single-star, catalogue        1.644349

    Panuzzo NSS astrometric, RAW      1.674700 +/- 0.0094

    L21-corrected refit    vs the zero-point-free a0/a1:    +1.9 uas = +0.11 sigma

Z5  THE RESIDUAL, bounded -- and what it costs
==============================================================================
  Three independent statements about how big the residual zero-point is
  after L21 is applied:
    (a) EB26's direct measurement on 40 astrometric ORBITAL solutions:
        Z = -0.0362 +/- 0.0053 mas vs the L21 median -0.0342 for the
        same sources -- a residual of -2.0 uas, 0.38 sigma from zero.
    (b) the scatter of L21 vs EB26's applied shift on the eight published
        pairs: median |diff| 2.0 uas.
    (c) Gaia BH3 against Panuzzo's zero-point-free a0/a1 parallax:
        residual +1.9 uas (+/- 17.6 uas, i.e. consistent with zero at 0.11 sigma).

  BOUND ADOPTED: the residual zero-point after L21 is <= 2 uas
  (the largest of the three, rounded up), against the 35 uas the correction itself
  removes at the median of this sample (the DR3 GLOBAL mean is 17 uas; this sample is
  fainter and redder than the all-sky average, so quoting the global number
  here would understate what is being removed by a factor two).

  WHAT IT COSTS IN COMPANION MASS.  dM2/M2 ~ -3 dvarpi/varpi, so a 2 uas
  residual costs:
    varpi  0.3000 mas ->  2.00 % on the companion mass
    varpi  0.5000 mas ->  1.20 % on the companion mass
    varpi  1.0000 mas ->  0.60 % on the companion mass
    varpi  1.6598 mas ->  0.36 % on the companion mass
    varpi  2.0000 mas ->  0.30 % on the companion mass
    varpi  5.0000 mas ->  0.12 % on the companion mass
    varpi 13.6000 mas ->  0.04 % on the companion mass

  For Gaia BH3 (varpi 1.66 mas) that is 0.36 % = 0.13 Msun on a 34.7 Msun
  companion -- against Panuzzo's own 0.82 Msun uncertainty.  The zero-point is
  no longer the dominant systematic once it is applied; it was only
  dominant while it was being ignored.
